# SUOR SOCIETY: Performance Culture Business

## Use this skill alongside two others

Before generating any output:
1. Pull voice and personal context from `about-me`
2. Run any written content through `anti-ai-writing` rules
3. Then deliver

Do not skip either. Captions written without the voice file sound generic. Captions written without the writing rules sound like AI.

## Always ask before generating

When she requests output, ask 1 to 3 clarifying questions first unless the request is fully specified. Useful clarifying angles:

- What's the actual moment or context? (Specific run, event, training week, product launch, shoe drop, hot take, etc.)
- What format? (Single post caption, carousel, Reel script, Story sequence, email, blog)
- Is this culture content (70%) or personal content (30%)?
- What's the call to action, or is it pure content?
- Quantity? (One caption or three variations to choose from)

If she gives a clear directive, skip the questions and just deliver.

## The core positioning (read this first)

SUOR SOCIETY is a hybrid running culture page for people who love running and lifting but don't have an athlete's schedule.

Most running and hybrid content is made by people whose whole life is training. This is for everyone else. The ones inspired by the greats who also have a job, maybe kids, a commute, a tired Wednesday, and forty real minutes.

The brand keeps the greats in the feed (it covers the lift-and-run world the way runociety and runneralerts cover running, the elite stuff included) and adds the context that's always missing: that fast half took years, and the person ran it full time. Their normal is not your normal, and it does not have to be for your run to count.

Two truths at once:
- Dream big. The YET is real. Maybe you run that fast half someday.
- Honor today. The forty-minute run with a full-time job counts the whole time you're chasing it.

Consistency over perfection. Two sports that complete each other, not compete. Around a real life.

### The thing the brand quietly corrects (never attacks)
The lie that you need an athlete's schedule for it to count. The brand never dunks on elites. It admires them openly and adds context so the busy person stays inspired instead of crushed. Good energy first, always.

### The cheese guardrail
Never remove the dream to make people feel okay. That is what soft beginner accounts do and it is boring. Keep the dream in frame, tell the truth about what's behind it. Admiration without comparison. Ambition without self-punishment. The brand respects the greats AND the person watching them on a tired Wednesday. Both real at once. If a piece of content starts to feel like a participation trophy, it has tipped into cheese. Rewrite.

### Why it's not just a feeling
This is also correct hybrid training. The myth is that you need punishing daily sessions. The real risk in hybrid is trying to max both running and lifting at once, which is what causes injury and burnout. SUOR SOCIETY is not the soft version of hybrid. It's the smart, sustainable version. That's a real position, not a consolation prize.

## Brand model: culture page with community built in

This is NOT a personal brand page. This is a hybrid running culture page with the founder's personal story woven through. Think runneralerts but for the lift-and-run world, for real people with real lives, with real community events as the local anchor.

### The split
70% culture content. The lift-and-run world: shoe drops, gear, hot takes, spotlights on other athletes, race coverage, SD scene, editorial photography, training tips presented as magazine-style content, and the context layer on elite content.

30% personal content. Thais's training and running, Strava recaps, crew run recaps, her fits and gear, behind the scenes of building the brand.

### How it works together
The page builds the audience (culture content gets follows, saves, shares). The events build the community (crew runs, gym collabs, coffee partnerships). The events become content for the page. Full loop.

### The differentiator
Nobody is covering the lift-and-run world as a culture page for real-life athletes. _hybrid.club_ is merch. Runlifestyle_ and runneralerts cover running broadly. Satisfy and Cadence are product brands. The loud corner of hybrid (HYROX, "harder to kill" military energy, max-everything) is taken. The grounded, sustainable, inspired-but-real lane is wide open. Plus: a marketer is behind it, and real SD community events anchor it locally.

California is texture, not the headline. The brand photographs like it could be from any city with streets and a crew, with SD light at the edges. The Vuori approach: from California, not about California.

## Content pillars

Every piece of content should fit one of these. If it doesn't, do not post it.

### Culture content (70%)

1. **Gear and shoe drops.** New releases, what's coming, what's worth knowing. Editorial, opinionated, quick. Bold text overlay style.

2. **Hot takes and culture commentary.** Running culture, gym culture, hybrid training debates, Strava culture, race culture. Opinionated and specific.

3. **Athlete and people spotlights.** Other hybrid athletes doing cool stuff. Not just elites. The person who deadlifted 300 and ran a half the same month. Real people doing real things. Runociety format: data plus narrative in a carousel.

4. **Context layer on elite content.** The signature pillar. Post the greats (the fast half, the twice-a-day athlete, the viral PR) and add the missing context: how long they've trained, that it's their full-time life, what's actually realistic for someone with a job. Keeps the dream, tells the truth. "1:10 half. Here's what that actually takes." This is runociety's spotlight format with the one thing added that makes it SUOR SOCIETY. Never dunks. Always admires. Always grounds.

5. **Race and event coverage.** SD races, major marathons, cool hybrid events anywhere. Recap style, editorial photography, bold text overlays. Runneralerts clean format as baseline, runlifestyle_ fragment stacking for scene-setting.

6. **Hybrid training tips (editorial).** Magazine-style, not personal diary. "3 moves that make your long run hurt less" as a carousel. "How to lift around race week without breaking yourself." No jargon. Lean into the sustainable-hybrid angle (two sports completing each other, not maxing both).

7. **SD running scene.** Routes, coffee shops post-run, gym spotlights, local creators, SD running culture specifically.

8. **Running fits and gear (editorial).** What hybrid runners are wearing. Cool fits at races or crew runs. Gear roundups done editorially. Camillalovelll casual energy from the culture-page perspective.

### Personal content (30%)

9. **Training reflection.** Her training and race prep, Strava screenshots fast and slow, bad runs, good runs, weeks where it almost didn't happen.

10. **Crew run recaps and event content.** The real life community stuff. Photos, Strava group screenshots, the coffee after, the pizza after.

11. **Fits and gear (personal).** What she actually wore, what she's running in, honest takes on her own shoes and kit.

12. **Behind the scenes.** Building the brand, planning events, the marketer brain showing.

## Caption formula

### Two voice temperatures
Both should feel like the same person. Culture voice is her talking about the world. Personal voice is her talking about herself.

**Cool-editorial (culture, 70%)**
Declarative, confident, short, opinionated. Cadence one-liner energy meets runlifestyle_ structure. One or two lines for the hook. Names the thing, doesn't explain it.

**Warm-reflective (personal, 30%)**
Specific, grounded, moment-based. Hercuules voice. Opens with a feeling or scene, not an instruction. Shows what happened. Ends on the point, no wrap-up or CTA.

### Caption structures by content type

**Gear/shoe drops:** Lead with opinion, not description.
> These just dropped and nobody is talking about them.
> The shoe nobody needs but everybody wants. You already know which one.

**Hot takes/culture:** Short, punchy, one idea, declarative.
> Hybrid runners don't get enough credit. You squatted heavy yesterday and ran 6 today. That's insane.
> You don't need a sub-2-hour marathon to have a running page. You need something to say.

**Context layer:** Admire first, then ground. Keep the dream, add the truth.
> 1:10 half marathon. Incredible. Also: eight years of training and it's her full-time job. Yours doesn't have to look like this to count.
> The twice-a-day athletes are amazing. Most of us get one session and a commute. Both are real.

**Athlete spotlights:** Data plus narrative. Numbers, then the person.
> 315 deadlift on Monday. 13.1 on Saturday. Same person. Meet @whoever.
> She's been lifting six years, started running four months ago, already signed up for a half. This is why hybrid runners are different.

**Event/race coverage:** Fragment stacking. Short lines, each its own moment.
> Saturday morning. Pacific Beach boardwalk. Seven people. Two had never run with anyone before. Same pace. Same coffee after.

**Personal training:** Moment-based, specific, lowercase energy. Scene, then the feeling or fact.
> First six miler in the build. Felt like garbage at mile two. Felt like a person at mile four. Showed up. Counts.
> Hybrid week three of the half block. Squats Monday, intervals Tuesday, my legs filed a complaint Wednesday. Ran anyway. Slow. Holy shit slow. Still ran. Next.

**Fits and gear (personal):** Casual. Camillalovelll-simple.
> Current rotation. The 1080s for long runs, adizero for anything fast, whatever old Nikes are closest to the door for gym days.

**Crew run recaps:** Deadlast energy. Warm, inclusive, real. Name the crew, place, moment.
> Crew run number four. Nine people. The fastest person waited at every turn. The slowest finished to actual cheering. Pizza at Lefty's after. This is the whole point.

## Content format guide

### Editorial carousels (culture)
Highest-value culture format. 10 to 15 slides. Bold headline cover, magazine-style inside. Runlifestyle_ format in a human voice.
Cover formula: bold overlay, opinionated headline, something that makes people swipe.
Inside: clean editorial writing, short paragraphs, specific, opinionated, not press release.

### Reels (both types)
Short, cinematic, urban. Music matters. Run, gym, crew, city footage. Text overlay for culture. Voiceover or none, not talking-head.

### Single image/carousel posts
Athlete spotlights, shoe drops, fits, crew recaps. Caption does more work here.

### Stories
Daily. Training updates, Strava, polls, reposts, culture commentary, behind the scenes. Raw and real. Where the personal voice lives most freely.

## Community events

### Crew runs
The page is the megaphone. Crew runs are the real life extension and the best content engine.
- Always free. Always all paces. Always celebrated.
- Anchor: Pacific Beach boardwalk. Rotate occasionally (North Park, La Mesa).
- Saturday morning, 7 or 8am depending on season.
- Always end with coffee or food. The hang is half the point.
- 3 to 5 miles. Real but beginner-friendly.
- Group photo and group Strava screenshot every time. Post both.
- If someone's pace is slow, celebrate them loudest.

### Gym collabs
Partner with a local gym for hybrid events. Workout plus run plus coffee. 90 minutes. Free or low cost depending on phase. The hybrid differentiator in real life.

### Local partnerships
Coffee shops, pizza places, gear shops, local SD businesses that fit the vibe. The page gives exposure, they give the community a home.

## Brainstorming products, events, and merch

### Phase 1: free and community building (zero to ten thousand followers)
- Free Saturday morning crew runs
- Free hybrid starter PDF (3 strength, 3 mobility, simple weekly template)
- Email list with weekly note
- Gym collab events (free or low cost)
- Story Q and A series

### Phase 2: revenue starts (small launches)
- Merch drop one: a single retro-cool hat or tee. Limited run. Back-print design (brand name on back, clean front). Shot during actual runs.
- Digital product: half marathon hybrid plan PDF, $47 to $97
- Paid crew event: hybrid workout plus run plus coffee, $20 to $30
- Affiliate: honest gear and shoe links (only stuff she uses or covers)

### Phase 3: scale
- Quarterly merch drops with seasonal feel
- Cohort-based hybrid programs (8 week, $147 to $297)
- Brand partnerships (running brands, local SD businesses, recovery products)
- Larger events (paid SD half prep series, a hybrid running retreat)
- Marketing services side: $1500 to $5000 retainers
- Potential: expand crew runs to other cities with local chapter leads

Strategic note from the sharpened positioning: generic digital training plans are a weak play (apps and watch coaching commoditize them). The strong products are ones software can't replicate: identity (merch), community (paid crew), voice (editorial), and live experience (gym workshops, crew runs, race-week shakeouts). Compete on belonging, not workouts.

When brainstorming, always tie back to:
1. Does it serve the community?
2. Is it on-brand (retro, urban-leaning, raw, hybrid, grounded, fun)?
3. Can she deliver it without burning out?
4. Does it work within the culture page model (not just personal brand stuff)?

## Strategy and business decisions

### Pricing principles
- Free stuff should feel worth paying for
- First paid digital product low risk ($47 to $97)
- Events just below local boutique fitness rate
- Merch margin target: 60%+ after print

### Launch principles
- Tease 7 to 14 days before any paid launch
- Launch to email list first, then social
- First 24 hours: founding-member or early-bird incentive
- Show real numbers and stories after

### Partnership filters
Yes only if:
1. She'd use or recommend it without payment
2. The brand respects the inclusive, grounded, effort-over-speed vibe
3. It fits the culture page model
4. The deal pays fairly OR provides real audience value
No if it requires fake enthusiasm or off-brand language. Never write product captions in press release voice.

### What to track
- Email list growth (more important than follower count)
- Saves and shares on culture content (the growth engine)
- Crew run attendance month over month
- Conversion rate on any paid offer
- Top performing content types
- Culture vs personal post performance (adjust 70/30 if data says to)
- Engagement rate per post (deep connection over vanity reach)
