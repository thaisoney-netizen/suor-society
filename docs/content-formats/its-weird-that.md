# Content format: "it's weird that"

A repeatable carousel / photo-overlay series. Each slide is one line that
starts "it's weird that ___" and points at a habit the run-and-lift-with-a-job
crowd catches themselves doing. Dry, wry, an "I can't believe we all agreed
this was normal" raised eyebrow. Never angry, never preachy.

Format reference in the wild: @lucydavis and similar pages. We borrow the
shape, not the words.

## Why it's ours and not a copy

The format is borrowed and trending. The difference is what goes inside it.
Every line has to be specific to running, lifting, and a real-life schedule.
Borrow the shape, fill it with our world.

This is the early-phase play on purpose: lean into easy, relatable, familiar
formats for reach while the specificity quietly does the brand work. As the
voice gets known, dial up the sharper, more distinctive stuff. The specificity
was building the different thing from day one.

## The rule (the copy test)

Never run the format with a generic line. If a random big fitness page could
post the exact same words, rewrite it until they couldn't. Swap in a detail
only our crowd would say: "30 minutes before work," "what was your pace," leg
day vs long run, the plan assuming you don't have a job.

## The logic check (does a line even fit the trend)

Say it out loud. The part after "weird that" has to name a real habit people
catch themselves in, so they read it and go "...yeah, why do I do that." That's
the eyebrow.

If it's just relatable-but-true (getting out of bed is hard), it is not weird,
it is a different kind of post. Those live as captions or stories, not in this
set. Dry eyebrow, not a complaint.

## Vetted line bank

### The tight 7 (all pass the copy test, good default carousel)

- it's weird that "what was your pace" became the first question, not "how was it"
- it's weird that 30 minutes before work is "just a short one"
- it's weird that lifting made me a better runner and nobody said so for years
- it's weird that the run fixes the mood we skip it for
- it's weird that someone's PR can ruin a run you were proud of
- it's weird that the plan always assumes you don't have a job
- it's weird that a rest day feels like getting away with something

Six that only our crowd would write, one pure-relatable anchor at the end
(rest day). That mix is the early-phase balance.

### Extended bank by theme

Job / real-life:
- it's weird that "I'll go after work" is either a promise or a lie and you never know which
- it's weird that the hardest part of the run was leaving the desk
- it's weird that we schedule the whole week and just hope the run fits

Regular runner:
- it's weird that "real runner" comes with a pace requirement
- it's weird that you have to earn the word athlete
- it's weird that "I only run for fun" gets said like an apology
- it's weird that walking a hill turned into a confession
- it's weird that we downplay the run we actually did
- it's weird that the back of the pack claps the loudest and gets filmed the least

Run + lift:
- it's weird that you're supposed to pick one, run or lift
- it's weird that leg day and long run got treated like enemies

Reflective / mindset (Lucy-style register, kept specific):
- it's weird that we think every run should feel exciting
- it's weird that "didn't feel like it" became a reason not to go

### Use sparingly (relatable but generic, max one per set as an easy-reach anchor)

- it's weird that a rest day feels like getting away with something
- it's weird that we treat strong and lean like the same goal

Both are widely relatable, so they pull reach, but any gym page could post
them. Never build a whole set out of these. One anchor at most.

### Does NOT fit the trend (keep as captions or stories, not "weird that" slides)

These are relatable and true, but nothing about them is backwards, so they
don't earn the eyebrow:

- the hardest part of the run was leaving the bed
- a 5am alarm is the only quiet you get all day
- you can PR on four hours of sleep and a deadline

## Visual

- Text in Sunrise orange, not the reference's yellow. The orange makes it ours
  the second someone scrolls past.
- Centered over a real candid photo (mid-zip, crew jumping, gym light, a wet
  street). Casual placement, slightly off dead-center, sitting over the person.
- Dark, urban, moody per the brand. Real photo, not a graphic.

## Caption

Light and self-aware. Example:

> been keeping a list. none of these should be hot takes

## Tone guardrails

No cheese, no inspirational-poster cadence, no dunking on anyone. Dry, knowing,
good energy. Keep the dream in frame, tell the truth underneath it. Full rules
live in the `anti-ai-writing` skill (cheese guardrail) and the voice comes from
`about-me`.
