# About Thais

## The basics

Early 30s. Lives in San Diego, Brazilian background. Marketer by day, hybrid athlete and community builder on the side.

Trains six days a week, full hybrid program. Has trained since she was 16, found her way into CrossFit, now does cross training. Picked up running as the newer challenge, the thing that pushed her edge. Runs solo and with one or two friends across Pacific Beach, North Park, South Park, La Mesa, and Balboa Park.

Just ran her first half marathon ever. Still relatively new to long-distance running. Already deep into strength and CrossFit-style training, so the running side is the newer edge. Lifting and running are not two separate goals competing for her time. They complete each other. She does not max both at once and break herself. She runs around a real life with a full-time job.

## The brand name

SUOR SOCIETY, locked in.

"Suor" is Portuguese for sweat, which ties to the tagline ("Run. Lift. Sweat.") and her Brazilian background. "Society" anchors the culture-page identity. English speakers will not instantly know "suor" means sweat, and that is fine. The meaning is part of the brand story, revealed over time, not something the audience needs to get on day one.

## The tagline

**Run. Lift. Sweat.**

Use this in bios, merch, content headers, and brand materials. This is the locked tagline (updated June 2026). It doubles as the site hero stack, so the tagline and hero are one and the same now.

Supporting line (audience-facing, still in use for hero copy and longer bios): "For the runner who lifts, the lifter who runs, and everyone figuring it out"

## The core idea (read this before anything)

Most running and hybrid content is made by people whose whole life is training. SUOR SOCIETY is for everyone else. The ones who love running and lifting but have a job, maybe kids, a commute, a tired Wednesday, and forty real minutes.

The brand keeps the greats in the feed. It admires the 1:10 half marathon, the people training twice a day, the elite accounts she actually loves like runociety. It does not remove the dream to make people feel okay. It adds the context that is always missing: that fast half took years, and the person ran it full time. Their normal is not your normal, and it does not have to be for your Tuesday to count.

Two truths held at once:
- Dream big. The YET is real. Maybe you run that 1:10 someday.
- Honor today. The forty-minute run with a full-time job counts the whole time you are chasing it.

That is the whole brand. Admiration without comparison. Ambition without self-punishment. Consistency over perfection.

The thing the brand quietly corrects (never attacks): the lie that you need an athlete's schedule for it to count. The brand never dunks on elites. It adds context so the busy person stays inspired instead of crushed. Good energy first, always.

This is also the science. Hybrid training done right is sustainable, not max-everything. Trying to maximize both running and lifting at the same time is what gets people hurt. SUOR SOCIETY is not the soft version of hybrid. It is the smart, sustainable version.

## The why

This is not a fitness influencer story. It started as something for herself, outside of work, and grew from there. The idea came directly from looking for running and hybrid accounts to follow and feeling left out by how elite everything was.

Three reasons it actually matters to her:
1. Mental health. Running and lifting clear her head in a way nothing else does.
2. She wants to feel strong and capable in her body. Not aesthetic-first. Capability-first.
3. She needed something that was hers. A space that wasn't her job, wasn't tied to performance reviews, wasn't anyone else's.

The community piece grew out of that. She has one or two run buddies and wants to build a real crew in San Diego. The culture page grew out of the community idea, because the lift-and-run world needs a media voice for real people with real lives, not just another elite personal brand.

## The brand model

This is a hybrid running culture page with personal content woven in. Not a personal brand. Not a pure media page. The split:

70% culture content. Shoe drops, gear news, hot takes on running and lifting culture, spotlighting other hybrid athletes, race and event coverage, SD running scene, editorial photography, hybrid training tips presented as magazine-style content, and the context layer on elite content (the greats, with the real story behind the numbers).

30% personal content. Her training and running, Strava screenshots, bad runs, good runs, crew run recaps, her fits and gear, behind the scenes of building the brand.

She is the voice, the curator, and the founder. Not the subject.

## Strava and data

She LOVES Strava. She posts maps, splits, screenshots, all of it. Pro-data, pro-sharing, pro-posting your run no matter how slow.

What she hates is pace shaming. Someone finishes a race and the first question is "what was your pace" instead of "how was it." This is a background value, not the headline. It comes up naturally when it comes up. The brand leads with good energy, not a stance against bad energy.

## Voice and how she actually talks

Mostly clean. Occasional "hell yeah" or "holy shit" for real emphasis, not as filler. She is not the "fuck yeah let's go" type. The original business plan doc had heavy swearing. That is not her voice.

Tone is confident but grounded. Playful without being silly. Reflective without being heavy. She can be dry. She can be direct. She does not perform.

She does NOT say:
- "You got this queen"
- "Period"
- "Slay"
- "Babe" as a default address
- "It hits different"
- Anything that sounds like a 19-year-old TikTok creator

She DOES say:
- "Hell yeah"
- "Holy shit" (sparingly)
- "Showed up"
- "Counts"
- "Not perfect, just consistent"
- "Out the door"
- "Did the thing"

## What she shares vs. keeps private

Open about: training reality, bad runs, Strava screenshots (even slow ones), weather complaints, fueling experiments, gear thoughts, where she ran and when, race nerves, the grind, outfit details, post-run coffee and pizza.

Lighter on: deep mental health processing, work specifics, romantic relationships, family stuff, anything that feels like a confessional. She references that running clears her head without making it the main story.

## Aesthetic

Urban-leaning even though she lives in San Diego. The brand should feel like it could be from any city with streets and a crew, but with SD light and coastal edges showing through. California is texture, not the headline (the Vuori approach: from California, not about California).

Performance-first shoes she actually likes (New Balance, adizero). Cool details on top: sunglasses, bandanas, retro-flavored fits that look good in motion. Not Tracksmith country club preppy. Not full streetwear. The middle ground where you look intentional but you also actually run hard.

Visual direction: lean into North Park and South Park streets for urban footage. When shooting PB or the coast, frame it gritty (early morning silhouettes, fog, headlamp, dark sky). Gym content is inherently urban (gym lighting, iron, concrete). Post-run at coffee shops and pizza spots in North Park reads more urban than a smoothie bar on the boardwalk.

Editing direction: darker end of the palette, Asphalt and Slate dominant, with grain and contrast. Sunrise orange and Rust red pop against dark backgrounds. Bold text overlays for culture posts (runneralerts style). Clean retro palette for personal and lifestyle posts. Mix depending on post type.

## Training rhythm

Six days a week. Full hybrid. Mix of CrossFit-style strength and running. Just finished her first half marathon block, so she's figuring out what's next.

Real talk: not every session is amazing. She has weeks where life happens. Some weeks it is six days, some weeks it is one, and both weeks she is still in. Showing up imperfectly is part of the personal 30%.

- Keep the greats in the feed and add the context that makes them inspiring instead of crushing
- Weave her personal training story through as the founder's thread
- Post Strava screenshots proudly, fast or slow

- Collab with a local gym, coffee shops, local SD businesses
- Spotlight other hybrid athletes, especially beginners and real people

- Make the page about herself primarily
- Pretend to be more advanced than she is
- Remove the dream to make people feel okay (no soft participation-trophy energy)

- Post content that feels generic, salesy, or influencer-coded
- Use motivational quotes
- Do the "look at me" hero edit
